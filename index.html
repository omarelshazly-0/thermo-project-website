💡 01

Project Idea

Compact Peltier-Based Air Cooling System

Our project focuses on the development of a localized, solid-state air cooler utilizing the thermoelectric (Peltier) effect. By applying DC power, the system creates a high-efficiency thermal gradient to chill incoming air without the need for chemical refrigerants or mechanical compressors. This eco-friendly design integrates precision heat sinks and high-speed fans to deliver a constant stream of cold air for personal workstations, providing a silent and sustainable alternative to traditional cooling methods.

📋 02

Planning

The plan was to build a vertical cooling unit using a PVC tube as the main body. We designed it to pull in room air through side holes, pass it over a cold heatsink powered by a Peltier chip, and push it out from the bottom. We also added a large fan at the top to make sure the heat stays away from the cooling area.

⚙️ 03

Execution Steps

A straightforward assembly process focused on good cooling and clean wiring.

1

Preparing the Tube: Cutting the PVC pipe and drilling a ring of holes in the middle for air intake.

2

The Cooling Sandwich: Attaching the Peltier chip between two heatsinks with thermal paste for the best cooling performance.

3

Wiring: Connecting the fans and the Peltier chip in a simple 12V circuit and insulating everything for safety.

4

Assembly: Putting the parts inside the tube and using a Sunboard disc to keep the hot and cold sides separated.

5

Final Touches: Closing the tube, mounting the fans, and adding small legs to help the air flow properly.

6

Testing: Powering it up and checking the temperature to make sure we're getting a steady flow of cold air.

📊 04

Results

The project works great! The air coming out is noticeably cooler than the room temperature. It’s quiet, portable, and does exactly what it’s supposed to do. It’s a solid example of how simple mechatronic parts can solve everyday problems like staying cool while working.
