Peltier-Based Portable AC Thermodynamics Project

Project Idea

Compact Peltier-Based Air Cooling System

Our project focuses on the development of a localized, solid-state air cooler utilizing the thermoelectric (Peltier) effect. By applying DC power, the system creates a high-efficiency thermal gradient to chill incoming air without the need for chemical refrigerants or mechanical compressors. This eco-friendly design integrates precision heat sinks and high-speed fans to deliver a constant stream of cold air for personal workstations, providing a silent and sustainable alternative to traditional cooling methods.

Planning

System Design & Component Selection

The planning phase involved selecting the optimal components to maximize cooling efficiency while keeping the design compact and affordable. The core of our system is the TEC1-12706 Peltier module, capable of generating a significant temperature differential when supplied with 12V DC. We designed a dual-zone thermal architecture: a cold side connected to an air duct with an intake fan, and a hot side with an aluminum heat sink and an exhaust fan to expel waste heat.

  • Peltier Module: TEC1-12706 (12V, 6A, max ΔT ≈ 66°C)
  • Heat Sinks: Aluminum fin-type, both cold & hot sides
  • Fans: 2× 80mm DC brushless fans (intake & exhaust)
  • Power Supply: 12V / 10A regulated DC PSU
  • Housing: Compact acrylic enclosure with air vents

Execution Steps

The build was carried out in six structured stages, from component assembly through final testing and enclosure. Each stage was documented to ensure reproducibility and to track any deviations from the original design specifications.

1

Mount Peltier Module

Applied thermal paste on both sides of the TEC1-12706 and clamped it between two aluminum heat sinks using screws to ensure maximum thermal contact and minimal interface resistance.

2

Hot-Side Cooling

Attached an 80mm exhaust fan to the hot-side heat sink to continuously dissipate waste heat, preventing thermal runaway and maintaining stable Peltier performance.

3

Air Duct Assembly

Constructed the cold-side air channel using acrylic panels. An 80mm intake fan draws ambient air over the cold heat sink, chilling it before it exits through the front vent.

4

Electrical Wiring

Connected the 12V regulated PSU to the Peltier module (observing correct polarity) and both fans. Used a connector block for neat and safe terminations.

5

Testing & Calibration

Used a K-type thermocouple with a digital meter to measure cold-side, hot-side, and ambient temperatures under steady-state conditions to verify ΔT targets were met.

6

Final Enclosure

Assembled the full housing, secured all components, applied insulation foam around the cold duct to minimize heat ingress, and performed final safety and performance checks.
